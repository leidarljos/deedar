What that output says
---------------------

Criterion 0.5 defaults are a 5 s measurement, 100 samples, and auto sampling.
Auto uses a flat sample when a linear sample would take more than twice the
measurement, and otherwise a linear one. It warns when the chosen mode is down
to one iteration at its smallest step. The flat recommendation is
floor(measurement / iteration), then down to a multiple of ten. The linear
warning names the time 100 one-iteration samples would take, and a smaller
sample count from the same measurement. The noise threshold those comparisons
use is 0.01: a 95% interval inside 1% of the mean is inside that threshold.

Receipt over a log of 5000, measured under the 8 s setting, mean 2.8669 ms
(interval 2.8616 ms to 2.8735 ms, half-width 0.21% of the mean). The run printed:

    Warning: Unable to complete 100 samples in 8.0s. You may wish to increase target time to 14.5s, enable flat sampling, or reduce sample count to 50.

5050 iterations is 100 * 101 / 2, the linear schedule at one iteration on the
first sample, and 5050 * 2.8669 ms is 14.5 s. That is why 8 s warns and why the
suggested sample count at 8 s is 50. At the default 5 s the linear schedule
would still take 14.5 s, which is more than twice 5 s, so auto selects flat.
50 ms a sample holds ceil(50 / 2.8669) = 18 iterations, and flat does not warn.

The same receipt group at 100 entries, mean 55.573 microseconds, collected
146k iterations in the 8 s and still reported 10% severe outliers (12 outliers
in 100, 2 of them mild). The log of 5000 reported 12% severe. The outlier rate
did not fall as the samples went from many iterations to one, so it is not a
reason to measure for longer than the sampler needs. Every 95% interval in the
receipt group was under 0.5% of its mean.

Export, one exporter, 100 deeds from a log of 5000, is the slowest iteration:
mean 64.193 ms, interval 64.148 ms to 64.248 ms, half-width 0.08% of the mean.
At a 5 s measurement and 100 samples, flat gives each sample 50 ms, which is
one iteration, and the warning would ask for about 6.4 s or fewer samples.
floor(5 / 0.064248) = 77, and the multiple of ten below that is 70. At 70
samples a 5 s measurement gives each sample 71.4 ms, two of those iterations,
which is the step at which the flat warning stops. The headroom before it
returns is an iteration of 71.4 ms, against a measured upper bound of 64.248 ms.

A flat-mode reading of only the slow export says 70 samples. That count still
warns, and the next run is why. The settings that follow both runs are
criterion's 5 s measurement for both groups, 100 samples for receipts, and 40
samples for export.

Receipts did not warn. The log of 5000 collected 1700 iterations in 5.0 s
(flat, 17 a sample) and reported no outliers. Its interval, 2.9247 ms to
2.9361 ms around 2.9304 ms, is 0.19% of the mean.

Exports of 10 deeds from a log of 1000 did warn. At about 2.2 ms and 2.7 ms
those iterations stay on linear sampling, and 70 samples is 2485 iterations on
that schedule, more than 5 s holds at one iteration each:

    Warning: Unable to complete 70 samples in 5.0s. You may wish to increase target time to 5.4s, enable flat sampling, or reduce sample count to 40.
    Warning: Unable to complete 70 samples in 5.0s. You may wish to increase target time to 6.8s, enable flat sampling, or reduce sample count to 40.

The time in each warning is how long the one-iteration schedule took, so
setting the measurement to it leaves the sampler on that step and the warning
returns. Both warnings name 40 samples. At 40 the linear schedule is
40 * 41 / 2 = 820 iterations, and 5 s / 820 is 6.1 ms, so an iteration under
that gets at least two on the first sample and the linear warning stops. An
iteration over 6.1 ms, up to the 125 ms one flat sample holds at 5 s / 40, is
measured flat with at least two iterations. The slow export, 64.1 ms, is in that range:
this run already measured it flat, 140 iterations across 70 samples, with no
warning, and 40 samples still leaves it two iterations (125 ms against 64 ms).

The export sample count is therefore 40.
